CON playbook - Technology is the source of good

Part 4 routes only, plus the Part 2/3 words you need to attack their case. Read the orange lines aloud.

Stasis · Disadvantages · Counter · Critique · Say this · They will say · Words · P4 recap

Proposition you reject

"Technology is the source of good in the world."

1. Force this point of stasis

Do not let them debate "is tech useful?" Force: is technology THE SOURCE of good, or only a tool?

  • Attack the word source. A source is the origin. A tool is used by someone else.
  • Shared question: Is technology the source of good in the world, or is it a tool under human values and institutions?
  • If they list vaccines and phones, say: those are grounds for usefulness, not for origin.

Before we start: our stasis is not whether technology can help. It is whether technology is the source of good. Source means origin. We reject that.

2. Direct disadvantages (Part 4)

Inherency

  • We already have technology. The status quo is not a world without tech.
  • In many ways things are already fine, or already improving, without treating tech as the origin of good.
  • Better fixes already exist: policy, ethics, education, law, democratic control. Those obstacles and current efforts weaken the need for their proposition.

Attack harms

  • They will say a world without tech is doomed. That harm is overstated.
  • People had care, justice, art, and solidarity before this stack of devices. Magnitude fails.
  • The worst harms today often sit inside tech use: war, surveillance, addiction. That is not "too little tech."

Attack solvency (not feasibility)

  • Solvency = does tech create good and solve the named harm? Feasibility = can we build it. Do not attack "tech is infeasible."
  • Tech does not create good. It does not solve war, inequality, or meaning.
  • It treats symptoms, not the disease. A vaccine is a tool. The source of the good is the choice to care and to share it.

3. Counter proposal (deliberative, not a question)

Say a sentence about what we should do. Do not ask "but what about AI?"

I think human values, institutions, and democratic control are a better source of good, because they decide whether a tool heals, harms, or is withheld.

We should treat technology as a means we constrain, not as the origin of good.

4. Critique of assumptions / flip the warrant

  • They assume technology equals the origin of good. That warrant is false.
  • Flip: if we want good, we must constrain tech (safety, privacy, war). Constraint is what makes outcomes good. That proves agents and values are the source.
  • "Source of" is an unqualified claim. No qualifier. Then extreme cases count: nuclear weapons, mass surveillance, addiction design.
  • Same grounds, new warrant (smoking-style): vaccines happened, therefore people and institutions directed a tool. Not: therefore tech is the source.

5. Ready-to-say lines (read aloud)

1 We reject the claim that technology is the source of good. Source means origin. Technology is a tool.

2 Keep stasis on the word source. Usefulness is not the same as origin.

3 We already live with technology. The status quo is not a stone-age vacuum. Inherency is weak for their side.

4 Better fixes already exist: policy, ethics, and education. We do not need to crown tech as the source of good.

5 A world without tech is not doomed. Care, law, and solidarity existed before this stack of devices. Their harm is overstated.

6 I am not saying technology is infeasible. I am saying it has no solvency on this claim. It does not create good.

7 Technology does not solve war, inequality, or meaning. It is a symptom-fixer, not the disease and not the cure.

8 I think human values, institutions, and democratic control are a better source of good, because they decide how any tool is used.

9 Flip their warrant: if we want good, we must constrain technology for safety, privacy, and war. Constraint proves the source sits with us.

10 Their claim has no qualifier. Then extreme cases count: nuclear weapons, surveillance, and addiction design.

11 Vaccines and networks are grounds for a useful tool. They are not grounds that artefacts are the origin of good.

12 Locate the source in responsible agents. Fund and constrain technology as a means, not as a moral origin.

6. They will say / you answer

  • They: Vaccines, water systems, and the internet prove tech is the source of good. You: Those are tools. People chose to fund, share, and regulate them. Solvency fails on the word source.
  • They: Without technology the world is doomed (disease, isolation, ignorance). You: Harm overstated. We already have tech, and good existed before this stack. Magnitude fails.
  • They: Tech uniquely scales solutions, so it must be the source. You: Scale is feasibility plus reach. Solvency is whether it creates good. It does not solve war, inequality, or meaning.
  • They: You are anti-technology / you want to ban tools. You: No. Counter proposal: treat tech as a means. Human values and democratic control are the better source.
  • They: Bad uses are user error, not the technology. You: Then you already concede agency. If users and states decide, they are the source, not the artefact.
  • They: We can qualify: a primary source, often, most of the time. You: The motion is unqualified. If they add a qualifier they have left the proposition. Extreme cases still land: nukes, surveillance, addiction.

They will run (short, for rebuttal only)

  • Indictment: without tech as a source of good, preventable harms stay (disease, isolation).
  • Solvency story: vaccines and networks solve those harms at scale.
  • You do not need a judge script. Hold stasis on source, then hit inherency, harms, solvency, and the counter proposal.

Words you need today

Point of stasis
The one shared question both sides must stay on. Yours: source, or tool?
Status quo
The current state of affairs. We already have tech.
Claim / grounds / warrant
Claim = what they want accepted. Grounds = support. Warrant = why those grounds prove the claim.
Qualifier
Limits a claim (often, most likely). Their motion has none, so extreme cases count.
Rebuttal
Answers an expected objection and still pushes your claim.
Harm
The damage they say the status quo does. Test the magnitude.
Solvency (vs feasibility)
Does the proposal actually solve the named harm? Feasibility is only whether you can do it. Attack solvency.
Inherency
Current efforts or facts that weaken the need for their proposition.
Direct disadvantages
Negative route: inherency, attack the harms, attack solvency (not feasibility).
Counter proposal
A different proposal. Must be deliberative, not a question.
Critique of assumptions
Attack warrants, grounds, or the indictment. You can flip the warrant.
Deliberative rhetoric
Forward-looking rhetoric about what to do next.

Part 4 recap - Negative

  • Three routes: direct disadvantages, counter proposals, critique of assumptions.
  • Also: inherency; attack harms; attack solvency (not feasibility).
  • Counter proposals are deliberative, not interrogative.
  • Flip the warrant.
  • The affirmative should be hard to beat. Still beat the word source.
  • Canvas rejected "change adds risk" as the Q2 key (score 1/2, no retake).

Part 2 - Model (short)

  • Six parts: Claim, Grounds, Warrant, Backing, Qualifier, Rebuttal.
  • Smoking examples: same grounds, different implicit warrant.
  • Qualifiers for extreme cases: most likely, 90%. They gave you none.

Part 3 - What they were taught

  • Indict the status quo; show solvency (not feasibility); be an example.
  • Agency = who must do it. If they admit agency, you win on source.
