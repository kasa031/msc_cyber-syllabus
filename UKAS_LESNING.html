Flipped classroom (short)

  • Lecture = homework (digital before class); class = shared activities + individual work with teacher help
  • Material comes ASAP (at least 1 week ahead). If it is late there may be a recording in class - OK, but the commitment still stands
  • Principles: professional and inclusive; breaks and food outside the room; arrive on time if you come

FAQ (Canvas) - teaching and practical

  • Attendance is not mandatory; you do not need to notify if you are away. Sessions are built for active participation / group work
  • Not hybrid teaching
  • Sessions are not recorded (safe to speak + learning through participation)
  • Research papers: search Oria via the OsloMet library oslomet.no/en/ul - sign in; use VPN / OsloMet network. Google Scholar often works too
  • Exam submission: Inspera via Studentweb - student.oslomet.no/hjemmeeksamen
  • Find your candidate number in Studentweb before the deadline
  • Video: CC captions in English only (speech recognition). Browsers: Chrome / Firefox / Edge / Safari. Allow pop-ups

Exam / essay + goal-posts

  • Individual essay (not group) = the exam: 5500-6500 words (not 12 000). Write continuously - iterative process
  • Not a typical literature review - assessment criteria are in Canvas
  • Essay = exam form. Check formal OsloMet requirements for the correct year: course info 2026 autumn (a Canvas link may still show 2024 - pick 2026 autumn)
  • Success: understand the material + written communication + time every week (like sculpture, not "code it once")
  • English: correct grammar and a fitting tone; plain English is OK; fancy language is not extra credit; avoid a patchwork style

Plagiarism / cheating (OsloMet - short)

  • Essay = individual exam: sharing the essay with others = a breach; plagiarism from sources is also forbidden
  • Electronic submission is checked against the internet + other exams (earlier years/courses); it catches synonym swaps and systematic rewriting
  • Process: report, notify + meeting, central board, decision
  • Sanctions: acquittal; annulled grade (retake the course); annulled + banned from all Norwegian higher education for up to 1 year
  • Experience in this course: cheaters often get the strictest sanction
  • Teachers help you write about other people's work - not write what they write
  • Rules: student.oslomet.no/en/cheating

Resit and retaking the course

  • Fail: a resit exam is available; it counts as a new exam attempt. Reasons for fail are in the assessment info document
  • After a fail you may keep working on the same essay you submitted (how much rewrite you need varies)
  • Pass, but want a better grade next year: you must submit a new essay (you cannot improve the same text the way failed students can on a resit)
  • Earlier approved mandatory work: valid only for the last two years; older work must be done again
  • Soft: local Essay assessment information

Essay requirements 2026 (short)

  • Topic: a research field in ACIT (your choice) - focus on the research, not mainly a technology
  • Perspectives (examples): methods; lab/experiment; philosophy of science; publishing/journal/conference; history; ethics/regulation; industry/society
  • The assessment criteria make some areas more mandatory - check that document
  • You are not an anthropologist: search/summarise/synthesise other people's knowledge; do not judge finding quality as an expert
  • Style: classic (Pinker) and/or narrative (short story); hybrid OK (documentary cut-scenes)
  • Style alone will not save thin content; best grade = rich content + well executed style
  • Weekly material starts the topics; you still need your own work + reading practice; writing exercises in class

AI rules (short)

  • Cheating: AI that literally writes parts of the essay = cheating (the exam = you write). Examples: AI writes from your ideas; AI "fixes" the draft; AI builds an essay from your material; AI restyles your paragraph into essay style
  • AI text is often flat/polished/business-like and gets weak grades; it also skips the learning of understanding and communicating research
  • Allowed / recommended: summarise/explain papers; group papers into categories; AI summarises YOUR draft/outline so you check coverage; discuss topic/structure; help finding material / common knowledge; learn grammar/writing from YOUR paragraph (you improve it yourself)
  • Grammarly and similar: not cheating if only on your own text; not recommended (the text often becomes unnecessarily "advanced")
  • Declaration at the end of the essay (after references): tools + activities, OR that you did not use AI. Outside the word count. AI is not required for a good grade. OsloMet: guidance on AI in student work

ACIT4280 - Privacy by Design

Prep: Will PETS solve the problem? (PET / TET)

Next natural reading after Ch. 1. Canvas page + flashcards (PET/TET oral defs, TetCat, Fritsch timeline). Pfitzmann/Hansen (pseudonyms) is a separate text, but belongs on the opacity side.

Canvas: Will PETS solve the problem? Zimmermann TetCat (arXiv:1507.04914) Zimmermann PDF
  • History: Chaum MIX (1981) - MixMaster = first practical PET. Fritsch timeline: 1970s debate; 1980s Mix/hiding/stego; 1990s MixMaster/credentials/commercialization; 2000+ regulation/compliance/projects
  • Two PET types: transparency tools (show processing: audit/log) vs opacity tools (hide identity/link: Tor, MixMaster, pseudonyms)
  • Opacity techniques: obfuscation, encryption, data separation, anonymization/pseudonymization, access + use restrictions
  • Hedbom TET: inform; give access to data/logic; counter-profiling
  • Pulls: transparency can collide with data minimisation (logging/visibility creates new privacy risks)
  • Ex-ante = before processing · Ex-post = after / in real time
  • TetCat (Zimmermann): Untrusted - Assertion; Trusted Ex Ante Read-only - Awareness / Interactive - Declaration; Trusted Ex Post Read-only - Audit / Interactive Intervention (collection/usage) / Remediation (+ modification/deletion)

Limitations of PETs in practice (short)

  • Three dimensions: technology, usability/ergonomics, maturity of IT investment decisions
  • Technical: crypto cost; TOR is slow; IDEMIX/U-Prove/Privacy ABCs are expensive; sticky policies are complex
  • Cost: PET/IT is often not the main driver (Ponemon) - privacy office, training, law/policy are larger; goal = cheapest sufficient compliance
  • ROPI (figure): Value of Privacy to Value at Risk to ROPI. Risk model: LB (breach list), PB (probability), CB (cost). Tools: LC (countermeasure list), ICB, efficiency. Formula: ROPI = PB*CB - ICB. Roadmap: Risk Model Road · Cost & Effect Lane (PET catalogue) · Empirics Road to a ROPI instrument (breach reporting = strongest empirics so far)

Privacy management: data, processes and administration (short)

  • Figure 1: Privacy protection = data flow (Collect/Store/Process/Transfer/Delete) + identity management (Identify/Authenticate/Link/rights/ownership + delegation)
  • Business process: data-flow models aligned with privacy policy/consent; role/access control; ID management over the full storage period
  • Formal policies: EPAL and XACML (machine-readable); "privacy policy" is ambiguous (also prose to the end user)
  • Sticky Policies (HP/Pearson): policy attached to encrypted data + trusted hardware + consent in the flow - complex across subcontractors
  • Housekeeping (GDPR DSR): labelling/metadata, privacy-friendly logs, policy/consent versioning, long-term ID management
